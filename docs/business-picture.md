# Business — Boxes on the picture and 360 video

Back to the [index](business.md).

Implemented in 1.2.6. Two things happen on the preview picture: everything that can be moved gets a visible box with the same handles, and 360° videos can be loaded, looked around in and exported as a normal flat video that follows the camera path the user sets.

## Features
- **Visible boxes**: every object that can be moved on the picture shows a thin box in the preview (today the only such objects are subtitle lines). Drag the box to move it; drag a corner to enlarge or shrink it. The box is a preview aid and is never exported.
- **Effect commands**: in the toolbar, next to Import, Add to timeline and the other actions, a separate group with **Align horizontally**, **Align vertically** and **Reset**. They work on the selected object(s) and are also in the Effects menu and the right-click menu.
- **360 videos**: a video recorded as a full 360° sphere (equirectangular) loads like any other video and is shown as a flat view you can look around in while it plays.
- **Look around**: drag the picture to turn the camera, use the mouse wheel to zoom in and out, also during playback.
- **Set POV**: "Set POV" saves the current view at the playhead as a point of view (POV). Between two POVs the camera moves smoothly from one to the other at a constant speed (a straight line, no easing). The exported video shows exactly this camera movement.

## Requirements

### Boxes, size and effect commands
- REQ-096 (implemented): Every movable object in the preview shows a visible box at the playhead; today these are the subtitle lines (REQ-089). The box is drawn only in the preview, never in the export, and can be hidden with View > Show boxes on the picture (on by default; the choice is a program setting). Clicking inside a box selects its object; Ctrl/Cmd+click and Shift+drag selection of REQ-090/091 are unchanged. Acceptance: with a subtitle line visible at the playhead a box is drawn around its text; the exported picture contains no box; turning the View option off hides every box.
- REQ-097 (implemented): An object can be moved by dragging its box (as in REQ-089, with the same limits: the box stays inside the picture) and enlarged or shrunk by dragging a corner handle of its box; the shape keeps its proportions and the opposite corner stays fixed. For a subtitle line the size is a factor of the preset's text size between 25% and 400%, kept per line like its position, shown the same way in the preview and the export (text still scales with the video height, REQ-017) and unchanged by an output format change (REQ-022). With several lines selected, all get the size of the line being dragged, like the position in REQ-092. One drag is one undo step. The cursor changes over the corners and over the box. Acceptance: dragging a corner of a line's box makes the text bigger; the same size appears in the export (centre and size within 2% of the picture height of the preview); the box never leaves the picture while enlarging; undo restores the previous size and position in one step; two lines selected, one enlarged: both get that size.
- REQ-098 (implemented): The toolbar has a group of effect commands, visually separated from the action buttons: **Align horizontally** and **Align vertically**. They move the selected object(s) so that their box is centred on the picture on that axis, keeping the other coordinate and the size (several selected lines: each is centred on its own). They are also in the Effects menu and in the right-click menu of the preview and of a subtitle line marker, with tooltips and shortcuts per REQ-071/072. They are greyed out with the reason "Select something on the picture first" when nothing is selected, and for a 360 camera view (nothing to align). One command is one undo step. Acceptance: a line moved to the lower-left, then Align horizontally: its centre is on the picture's vertical centre line and its height position is unchanged; same for vertical; two selected lines both change; undo restores in one step; at 800 pixels wide the commands are reachable (in the "»" menu if they do not fit).
- REQ-099 (implemented): **Reset** (toolbar, Effects menu, right-click menus) brings the selected object(s) back to their original size and position: for a subtitle line the style's usual place and size 100% (it extends "Reset position" of REQ-089/092, which keeps working); for a 360 clip being viewed it returns the live view to the front, level, with the default zoom (REQ-101), without removing the saved POVs. Greyed out with a reason when nothing is selected or when everything selected is already original. One undo step for subtitle lines (the live 360 view is not part of the project and has no undo). Acceptance: after moving and enlarging a line, Reset gives the preset position and 100% size; with three selected lines all three are reset in one step; on a 360 clip it returns the view to the front and keeps the POVs.

### 360 video
- REQ-100 (implemented): videolore recognises a 360° video (equirectangular projection, one picture, not stereo 3D) when its file carries the spherical-video information, and marks the media item "360°". A 360 clip is then shown through a virtual camera instead of the whole flat picture. In Clip details every video clip has the switch "Treat as 360° video", so the user can turn it on for a 360 file without the information (for example a plain 2:1 picture) or off for a false recognition; the choice is saved in the project and applies to the clip's source. Files with no spherical information are normal videos by default. Acceptance: a test file with spherical metadata imports with the "360°" tag and previews as a camera view; the same file without metadata previews flat until the switch is turned on; the switch survives save and reopen.
- REQ-101 (implemented): While a 360 clip is shown in the preview, dragging the picture turns the camera (left/right and up/down; the picture follows the pointer), the mouse wheel (or pinch) changes the zoom between 30° and 110° of horizontal view (default 90°). It works while paused and during playback. The camera cannot turn past straight up or down and turns freely around. This live view is a viewing aid: it is not saved by itself and does not change the exported video until it is set as a POV. With POVs on the clip a manual drag takes over from the camera path until the user seeks, presses play again or presses Reset. A subtitle box under the pointer is dragged instead of the camera. Acceptance: dragging 100 pixels to the right turns the view by the same angle in every test, in preview and during playback; pitch is clamped at ±90°; wheel zoom stays in 30°–110°; the picture is never distorted (a straight line in the world stays straight in the view).
- REQ-102 (implemented): **Set POV** (a button in the preview controls, and in the Playback and Effects menus and the right-click menu of the preview) stores the current view (direction and zoom) as a point of view at the playhead on that 360 clip. Each POV has a marker on the timeline (tooltip with time and angles; click opens a small popup with the time, the direction and zoom as numbers, and Remove). Between two POVs the camera moves in a straight line in time from one view to the next, turning the short way round; before the first POV and after the last one the camera stays on it. A clip with a single POV keeps that view throughout. Setting a POV at a time that already has one replaces it. Up to 200 POVs per clip. The path stays continuous when the clip is edited: after a split, a trim or a cut of the clip's start the view at each edge is the one the path had there; copies and pasted clips keep their POVs; POVs that fall outside the kept part are hidden, not lost, while the source range can be restored by trimming back. Each Set POV, move, edit and Remove is one undo step. Acceptance: POVs at 0 s (yaw 0°) and 10 s (yaw 90°): at 5 s the view is at 45° (±1°) in preview and export; from 350° to 10° the camera passes through 0°, not through 180°; split at 5 s gives two clips whose edge views match; undo of Set POV removes it and its marker; the view before the first POV equals the first POV.
- REQ-103 (implemented): Export renders every 360 clip as a normal flat video of the camera path (what the preview shows), frame by frame and at the same angles as in the preview, together with fades, subtitles, music and volumes; the export is never stream copy (REQ-023) when a 360 clip is on the timeline, and the exported file is never a 360 video. For the automatic output format (REQ-022) a 360 clip counts as a flat picture of 1920x1080 pixels; if the project has only 360 clips the output is 1920x1080 (assumption), otherwise it follows the other clips with the usual rule of REQ-022 (the lowest pixel count wins, so a 360 clip next to smaller normal clips gives their format), with the usual letterboxing. Exporting a moving camera is slow (about one third of real time on the reference computer; see the limitations). "Export: Faster" (REQ-055) and the output check (REQ-063) apply as for any re-encoded clip. Acceptance: the exported picture at 3 sample times matches the preview of the same times (view direction within 1°); the exported file is flat 16:9 (or the format of REQ-022); a project with one 360 clip and one normal clip exports with both correctly framed; with Faster on, the check still passes or the normal method is used.
- REQ-104 (implemented): 360 video stays comfortable to use. Looking around, playing and scrubbing a 360 clip keep each interface frame under 200 ms, also while an export and subtitle generation run (REQ-056), at the sizes users have (up to 8K x 4K equirectangular, 30 fps). Large 360 sources get the preview copy of REQ-038/051 under the same rule (the camera view is made from the copy; export always uses the original). A 360 file larger than the computer can handle in the preview gives a clear message and still exports; videolore never crashes. Acceptance: an automated look-around drag during an export and subtitle generation keeps every frame within 200 ms on a 4K x 2K and an 8K x 4K test file; an unsupported size shows the message.

## Key decisions
- **What you see is what you export**: the exported file is a normal flat video that follows the camera path. Keeping the 360 format in the export is not offered, because videolore is aimed at short social clips.
- Moving and enlarging use the same box and handles for every object, so there is one thing to learn. For now only subtitle lines can be moved; new movable objects will get the box automatically.
- **Align** means centring on the picture, **Reset** means the original size and position; both are single undo steps.
- The camera path is a straight line in time between points of view, with no easing, as requested; the path is kept per clip, so editing the clip never makes the camera jump.
- A 360 video is recognised from the file's own information, so a normal 2:1 video is never distorted by mistake; the user can override it per clip.
- The live look-around is not saved; only Set POV changes the project and the export.
- The project file format changes again (see [Editing](business-editing.md), REQ-105).

## Impact on existing features
- REQ-070 (toolbar): the 12 commands now come with the effect commands group; at 800 pixels wide everything stays reachable through the "»" menu.
- REQ-069 (menus): the Effects menu gets Align horizontally, Align vertically and Reset; the View menu gets Show boxes on the picture; the Playback menu gets Set POV.
- REQ-089 / REQ-092 (subtitle position): dragging is done on the visible box; the size joins the position as a per-line value; "Reset position" now also resets the size.
- REQ-022 / REQ-023 (export): 360 clips are always re-encoded and count as 1920x1080 for the automatic output format.
- REQ-038 / REQ-051 (preview copies) and REQ-056 (responsiveness): apply to 360 clips, see REQ-104.
- REQ-071 / REQ-072: new shortcuts and tooltips for the three effect commands and Set POV; new greyed-out reason "Select something on the picture first".
- REQ-078 (Clip details): the "Treat as 360° video" switch.
- REQ-076 / REQ-077 (popups and markers): a POV popup and POV markers on the timeline of the 360 clip.
- REQ-029: projects from 1.2.5 and earlier open with the same result (REQ-105).

## Known limitations
- Only equirectangular, single-picture 360 video is supported (no stereo 3D, cube maps or partial 180° video); the spatial (ambisonic) sound is played as it is and does not follow the camera.
- The exported picture is flat; no roll (tilt) of the camera.
- Keyboard-only control of the 360 camera is not available.
- Exporting a 360 clip with a moving camera runs at about one third of real time (a 30 s clip takes about 90 s); the interface stays responsive meanwhile (REQ-104). No faster speed is promised.
- Reset on a subtitle line also clears its size, not only its position.
- Subtitle lines overlapping in time can overlap on screen after moving or enlarging; text edited after enlarging can push the box past the edge in the export (as in REQ-089).

## Out of scope
- Exporting 360 video; stereo 3D, cube maps, 180° VR video, headsets and motion sensors.
- Easing curves or other speeds between POVs; camera roll; animating the zoom separately from the POVs.
- Boxes for rotating objects, free-aspect resizing, snapping or guides, keyboard nudging.
- New object types to place on the picture (titles, stickers, overlays).
- Subtitles fixed to a point on the sphere.
