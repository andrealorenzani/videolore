# Business — Boxes on the picture and 360 video

Back to the [index](business.md). Later 360 work (easing, roll, keyboard, sphere subtitles, spatial sound) is in [360 video](business-360-video.md); Change aspect, Scale and Move are in [Frame](business-frame.md).

## Features
- **Visible boxes**: every object that can be moved on the picture (subtitle lines, text items) shows a thin box in the preview; drag it to move, drag a corner to resize. The box is never exported.
- **Context Menu**: a toolbar group, shown only when it has something to act on: a selected subtitle line or text item (Align horizontally, Align vertically, Reset), or the playhead on a 360 clip (Set POV, Reset view).
- **360 videos**: a full 360° sphere (equirectangular) loads like any video and is shown as a flat view you can look around in, also during playback.
- **Look around**: drag the picture to turn the camera; the mouse wheel zooms between 30° and 110°.
- **Set POV**: saves the current view at the playhead as a point of view; between two points of view the camera moves at constant speed in a straight line.
- **360 export**: for a project whose clips are all 360, the Export dialog offers "360 video" (the whole sphere) or "Flat camera view" (the camera path as seen in the preview).
- **Text and subtitles on 360**: an item added at the playhead stays at the point of the sphere the camera looked at.

## Requirements

### Boxes, size and Context Menu
- REQ-096 (implemented): Every movable object in the preview shows a visible box at the playhead; today these are the subtitle lines (REQ-089) and text items. The box is drawn only in the preview, never in the export, and can be hidden with View > Show boxes on the picture (on by default; a program setting). Clicking inside a box selects its object; Ctrl/Cmd+click and Shift+click add it to the selection (REQ-090), and Shift+drag on the timeline selects a time range (REQ-091). Acceptance: with a subtitle line visible at the playhead a box is drawn around its text; the exported picture contains no box; turning the View option off hides every box.
- REQ-097 (implemented): An object can be moved by dragging its box (the centre of the box stays on the picture, as REQ-089 and REQ-117) and enlarged or shrunk by dragging a corner handle (for subtitle lines; a text item changes its width only, REQ-116); the shape keeps its proportions and the opposite corner stays fixed. For a subtitle line the size is a factor of the preset's text size between 25% and 400%, kept per line like its position, shown the same way in the preview and the export and unchanged by an output format change (REQ-022). With several lines selected, all get the size of the line being dragged, like REQ-092. One drag is one undo step. The cursor changes over the corners and the box. Acceptance: dragging a corner of a line's box makes the text bigger; the same size appears in the export (within 2% of the picture height of the preview); the box never leaves the picture while enlarging; undo restores the previous size and position in one step; two lines selected, one enlarged: both get that size.
- REQ-098 (implemented): The Context Menu (REQ-106) shows **Align horizontally** and **Align vertically** when at least one subtitle line is selected. They centre the selected lines' boxes on the picture on that axis, keeping the other coordinate and the size (several selected lines: each is centred on its own). They are also in the Edit menu (greyed out when nothing is selected, REQ-069) and in the right-click menus, with tooltips and shortcuts (REQ-071, REQ-072). One command is one undo step. Acceptance: a line moved to the lower left, then Align horizontally: its centre is on the picture's vertical centre line and its height position is unchanged; same for vertical; two selected lines both change; undo restores in one step; with no subtitle line selected the two commands are not shown in the Context Menu.
- REQ-099 (implemented): **Reset** (Context Menu, Edit menu and right-click menus) brings the selected subtitle line(s) back to the style's usual place and size 100% (it extends "Reset position" of REQ-089/092). With the playhead on a 360 clip it returns the live view to the front, level, with the default zoom (REQ-101), without removing the saved POVs. Greyed out with a reason when nothing applicable is selected or everything is already original. One undo step for subtitle lines (the live 360 view is not part of the project and has no undo). Acceptance: after moving and enlarging a line, Reset gives the preset position and 100% size; with three selected lines all three are reset in one step; on a 360 clip it returns the view to the front and keeps the POVs.
- REQ-106 (implemented): The **Context Menu** is a toolbar group, visually separated from the action buttons. It is shown only when it has something to act on, and not shown (not greyed out) otherwise. It is shown when (a) at least one subtitle line or text item is selected: Align horizontally, Align vertically and Reset (REQ-098, REQ-099, REQ-115); or (b) the playhead is over a 360 clip: Set POV (REQ-102) and Reset view (REQ-099). Set POV is shown even when nothing is selected, as it acts on the playhead. The same commands stay in the menus (REQ-069). At narrow widths the buttons follow REQ-070 (the "»" menu). Acceptance: with nothing selected on a normal clip the group is not visible; selecting a subtitle line shows the three commands; moving the playhead onto a 360 clip shows Set POV; selecting a text item shows the group with the three commands.
- REQ-117 (implemented): A text item or a subtitle line can be dragged past the edges of the picture, to place it exactly at an edge or partly outside it. The centre of its box stays on the picture, so the box can always be grabbed again (assumption). The part outside the picture is not drawn, in the preview or in the export. Positions outside the picture are stored as fractions of the picture (REQ-089) and are valid project values (REQ-123). On a 360 clip the limit is the look-around view (REQ-112): a box is not dragged out of the view. Acceptance: a subtitle line dragged until its left edge is 30% of its width outside the picture is cut at the picture edge in the preview and in the export, the two within 2% of the picture height; dragging its centre past the right edge stops it on the edge; one undo restores its place; a text item dragged the same way behaves the same.
- REQ-146 (implemented): A subtitle line or text item partly outside the picture (REQ-117) stays resizable through its visible part. Its handles are drawn and grabbed where the box is visible, and the visible edge of a subtitle line's box is a resize handle as its corners are (proportional, REQ-097, same 25%-400% limit). A text item keeps its width-only handle (REQ-116) on the visible part of its right edge. One drag is one undo step. Acceptance: a subtitle line with its centre on the right edge of the picture can be enlarged by dragging its visible top or bottom edge, and the enlargement is proportional; a text item whose right edge is outside the picture still has its width handle, at the picture edge; one undo restores each change.

### 360 video
- REQ-100 (implemented): videolore recognises a 360° video (equirectangular projection, one picture, not stereo 3D) when its file carries the spherical-video information, and marks the media item "360°". A 360 clip is shown through a virtual camera instead of the flat picture. There is no switch in Clip details (removed in 1.4.0): a clip is 360 only from its file's spherical information, so a flat video is never shown as 360; Clip details shows the "360°" tag as information only. Acceptance: a test file with spherical metadata imports with the "360°" tag and previews as a camera view; the same file without metadata previews flat and Clip details offers no 360 option for it.
- REQ-101 (implemented): While a 360 clip is shown in the preview, dragging the picture turns the camera (left/right and up/down; the picture follows the pointer); the mouse wheel (or pinch) changes the zoom between 30° and 110° of horizontal view (default 90°). It works while paused and during playback. The camera cannot turn past straight up or down and turns freely around. This live view is a viewing aid: it is not saved by itself and does not change the exported video until it is set as a POV. With POVs on the clip a manual drag takes over from the camera path until the user seeks, presses play again or presses Reset. A subtitle box under the pointer is dragged instead of the camera. Acceptance: dragging 100 pixels to the right turns the view by the same angle in every test, in preview and during playback; pitch is clamped at ±90°; wheel zoom stays in 30°–110°; the picture is never distorted (a straight line in the world stays straight in the view).
- REQ-102 (implemented): **Set POV** (a button in the preview controls, in the Playback menu, in the Context Menu (REQ-106) and in the right-click menu of the preview; not in the Effects menu, REQ-069) stores the current view (direction and zoom) as a point of view at the playhead on that 360 clip. Each POV has a marker on the timeline (tooltip with time and angles; click opens a small popup with the time, direction and zoom as numbers, and Remove). Between two POVs the camera moves in a straight line in time from one view to the next, turning the short way round; before the first POV and after the last one the camera stays on it. A clip with a single POV keeps that view throughout. Setting a POV at a time that already has one replaces it. Up to 200 POVs per clip. The path stays continuous when the clip is edited: after a split, a trim or a cut of the clip's start the view at each edge is the one the path had there; copies and pasted clips keep their POVs; POVs that fall outside the kept part are hidden, not lost. Each Set POV, move, edit and Remove is one undo step. Acceptance: POVs at 0 s (yaw 0°) and 10 s (yaw 90°): at 5 s the view is at 45° (±1°) in the preview; from 350° to 10° the camera passes through 0°, not through 180°; split at 5 s gives two clips whose edge views match; undo of Set POV removes it and its marker; the view before the first POV equals the first POV.
- REQ-103 (implemented): For a project whose video clips are all 360 clips, the Export dialog (REQ-122) asks which video to make: **360 video** or **Flat camera view**. The automatic output format stays the default (REQ-022). A **360 video** keeps the whole sphere in equirectangular form (not the camera view) and carries the spherical-video information, so 360 players show it as a sphere; the viewer chooses where to look; the camera path (REQ-102) and the live view (REQ-101) are not applied. Fades, subtitles, text items (REQ-111, REQ-112), music and volumes are applied to the sphere, so subtitles and text keep their places. A **Flat camera view** is a flat video of the camera path as shown in the preview, at the same angles; a project that mixes 360 and flat clips is exported only as a flat camera view (REQ-122). Output format: REQ-022 with the 360 clips' own pixel sizes for a 360 video; the 1.2.6 rule (360 counts as 1920x1080) for a flat camera view. Stream copy follows REQ-023; a 360 clip is always re-encoded for a flat camera view. Speed-first (REQ-266 in [360 video](business-360-video.md)); "Export: Faster" (REQ-055) and the output check (REQ-063) apply as for any re-encode. Acceptance: an all-360 project shows the 360 choice, a project without 360 clips and a mixed project do not (REQ-122); a 360 video with a text at yaw 30° has the text at yaw 30° (±1°) at three sample times; the output file carries the equirectangular spherical information; with Faster on, the check passes or the normal method is used.
- REQ-104 (implemented): 360 video stays comfortable to use. Looking around, playing and scrubbing a 360 clip keep each interface frame under 200 ms, also while an export and subtitle generation run (REQ-056), at the sizes users have (up to 8K x 4K equirectangular, 30 fps). Large 360 sources get the preview copy of REQ-038/051 (the camera view is made from the copy; export always uses the original). A 360 file larger than the computer can handle in the preview gives a clear message and still exports; videolore never crashes. Acceptance: an automated look-around drag during an export and subtitle generation keeps every frame within 200 ms on a 4K x 2K and an 8K x 4K test file; an unsupported size shows the message.
- REQ-105 (implemented): Projects from 1.2.5 and earlier open in 1.2.6 with the same result (no 360 clips, every subtitle line at 100% size). Projects saved by 1.2.6 also store the subtitle line sizes, the "360°" switch of each clip and the points of view (REQ-096 to REQ-104); older versions refuse them and leave the file untouched (REQ-029). Damaged or hostile values (size outside 25%-400%, angles or zoom out of range, more than 200 POVs, POV times outside the clip, unknown references) are refused or corrected without a crash (REQ-057). Acceptance: a 1.2.5 example project renders and exports as before; a 1.2.6 file is refused by the previous version's check; the hostile values give no crash.
- REQ-112 (implemented): On a 360 clip, a text item or a subtitle line added at the playhead is placed at the point the camera looks at that moment (the centre of the view) and stays at that point of the 360 world. A subtitle line placed this way is drawn as plain white text (no preset box, no word highlight; fixed in 2.0.4, REQ-257). When the camera turns, the item does not move: it is drawn at its new place in the view, computed with the projection of the view so that straight lines stay straight, and it is not drawn while its point is outside the view. Its size is fixed in the world, so zooming the view makes it bigger or smaller like the scene. Dragging its box in the look-around view moves it to another point of the view (the box stays inside the view). In the 360 export (REQ-103) it is at the same point of the sphere. Subtitle lines generated automatically on a 360 clip keep the view-fixed default place (assumption). Acceptance: a text added with the view at yaw 30° and pitch 10° is drawn 40° left of the centre after the camera turns 40° to the right (±1°); turning back puts it at its first place (±1°); in the 360 export at three sample times its direction is yaw 30° and pitch 10° (±1°).

## Key decisions
- For a project with 360 clips the user chooses at each export between a 360 video and the flat camera view; this is the 360 choice of the Export dialog, shown only when all video clips are 360 clips (REQ-122).
- Text items and subtitle lines added on a 360 clip are anchored to a point of the sphere, so they do not move when the camera moves.
- The Context Menu is shown only when it has something to act on. Set POV acts on the playhead, not on a selection, so it is shown without a selection.
- The camera path is a straight line in time between points of view, with no easing; the path is kept per clip, so editing the clip never makes the camera jump.
- A 360 video is recognised from the file's own information, so a normal 2:1 video is never distorted by mistake; there is no per-clip override.
- The live look-around is not saved; only Set POV changes the project.
- Align means centring on the picture; Reset means the original size and position; both are single undo steps.

## Known limitations
- Only equirectangular, single-picture 360 video is supported (no stereo 3D, cube maps or partial 180° video). Spatial sound follows yaw and pitch only (see [360 video](business-360-video.md)).
- The camera has no roll; keyboard control of the 360 camera is in [360 video](business-360-video.md).
- A 360 video export does not include the camera path; use the flat camera view for a guided movement.
- A project that mixes 360 and flat clips can only be exported as a flat camera view.
- Text items and subtitle lines placed on a 360 clip are not shown while their point is outside the view; there is no indicator showing where they are.
- Subtitle lines generated automatically on a 360 clip keep the view-fixed place and move with the camera (assumption).
- A 360 video export ignores Change aspect, Scale and Move (the flat camera view applies them).
- A video without spherical information is always flat; the "360°" switch was removed in 1.4.0.
- The 360 marker of a 360 video export is written for MP4 and MOV; Matroska and WebM only when the check passes (see [360 video](business-360-video.md)).

## Out of scope
- Stereo 3D, cube maps, 180° VR video, headsets and motion sensors.
- Animating the zoom separately from the POVs; boxes for rotating objects; free-aspect resizing of boxes.
- Shapes on the picture (PNG picture items and overlap media are in [Effects and frame options](business-effects-frame.md) and [Overlap media](business-overlap.md)).
