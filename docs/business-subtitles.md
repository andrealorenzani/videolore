# Business — Subtitles

Back to the [index](business.md).

## Features
- Automatic subtitles generated on this computer from the speech in the video (never from the music), in the background with progress and cancel.
- Three subtitle quality levels (Fast, Balanced, Most accurate), chosen in Settings; the recommended one depends on the computer (see [Settings, performance and safety](business-performance.md)).
- The speech model of the chosen level is downloaded once, after a dialog that shows its size (about 32 MB for Fast, 190 MB for Balanced, 574 MB for Most accurate) and licence and asks for consent; alternatively the user can provide the model file. After that, everything works offline.
- On processors too old for the speech engine, a clear message replaces automatic generation; lines can still be added by hand.
- Language detected automatically, with a manual override.
- Subtitle lines can be added, deleted, re-worded and re-timed; all changes can be undone.
- At least three social-media styles, including word-by-word highlight, visible in the preview and burned into the exported video.
- Subtitles stay attached to the speech they belong to when the timeline is edited.
- (1.2.0) Subtitles can be created for one clip only or for the whole video (see [Window, menus and effects](business-ui.md), REQ-082).
- (1.2.0) When a video already contains subtitles, videolore asks whether to use their text or to recognise the speech; the video's text is then matched word by word to the speech, so the word-by-word highlight follows what is said (approximately; see the known limitations).
- (1.2.0) Subtitles can be saved as a `.srt` file.
- (1.2.0) Each subtitle line can be dragged to its own place on the picture; several lines can be selected (Ctrl/Cmd+click, or Shift+drag on the timeline) and moved to the same place together.
- (2.0.3) Subtitle lines and text items are placed together: overlapping lines and items partly outside the picture are warned about, "Move inside the picture" brings them back, stacked boxes can each be picked with repeated clicks, a status hint names the selection gestures, and a selection of lines and texts moves as one group.

## Requirements

### Creating and editing
- REQ-014 (implemented): Subtitles are generated offline from the video audio only (not the music), in the background with progress and cancel. The speech model of the chosen subtitle quality level (REQ-052) is downloaded once after a consent dialog showing its size and licence, or provided by the user as a local file; each model has its own consent; afterwards everything works offline. These downloads are the only network access of the product.
- REQ-015 (implemented): The subtitle language is detected automatically and can be overridden by the user; a project has one language.
- REQ-016 (implemented): Subtitle lines can be added, deleted, re-worded and re-timed, and all these actions can be undone.
- REQ-017 (implemented): At least 3 social-media style presets are available, including word-by-word highlight; a project uses one preset; subtitles are visible in the preview and burned into the export; text size scales with the video height so it is readable in both vertical (9:16) and horizontal video.
- REQ-018 (implemented): Subtitles are tied to the speech in the source: cutting a segment removes its subtitles, moving or copying a segment moves or duplicates them, trimming hides those outside the kept range, splitting keeps them.
- REQ-044 (implemented): Regenerating subtitles asks for confirmation before replacing lines the user has edited, also when regenerating at a different subtitle quality level.
- REQ-085 (implemented): Subtitles as an effect (REQ-082). **On a clip**: subtitles are created, by recognition or by reusing the video's own subtitles (REQ-087), only for the part of the source that the clip uses; lines elsewhere are untouched. **On the whole video**: for every clip (as "Generate subtitles"). If lines already exist in that range and some were edited by hand, videolore asks first (REQ-044). Only one subtitle job runs at a time; meanwhile the command is greyed out with "Subtitles are being created". Acceptance: (a) applying it to clip 2 of 3 creates lines only in clip 2's range; (b) the lines of clips 1 and 3 are unchanged; (c) only clip 2's audio range is passed to recognition.

### Subtitles already in the video
- REQ-086 (implemented): When media is analysed, videolore records the text subtitle tracks it contains (SRT, ASS/SSA, MP4 timed text, WebVTT) with their language and title. Picture-based tracks (DVD, Blu-ray PGS, DVB) are ignored. Media imported before 1.2.0 is checked again in the background when a project is opened. The media item's tooltip reads "Contains subtitles (<language>)". Acceptance: an MKV with an SRT track and an MP4 with timed text are reported with their language; an MKV with only a picture-based track reports none; a damaged or hostile track description never crashes videolore (REQ-057).
- REQ-087 (implemented): When subtitles are about to be created (REQ-085) and at least one source involved has a usable subtitle track, a dialog asks "This video already contains subtitles. Use their text, or recognise the speech?" with the buttons "Use the video's subtitles", "Recognise speech" and "Cancel"; when there are several tracks, a list (language and title) picks one. **Use**: the lines take their text from the track, only for the range being processed; formatting and position tags are removed; a cue on several lines becomes one line; the text is cleaned like typed text; the words are then matched to the speech (REQ-095); the track's text is never replaced by recognised words; the project language is taken from the track unless the user chose one (REQ-015) and is passed to recognition; the lines count as not edited and can be edited (REQ-016). **Recognise**: the usual recognition (REQ-014, REQ-052, REQ-053). Sources without a track always use recognition. **Cancel**: nothing changes and no undo step is added. The question is asked every time. Acceptance: (a) with an SRT track and Use, the timeline lines have exactly the cues' text (tags removed) for the clip's range; (b) a cue with `{\an8}` or `<i>` shows plain text; (c) Recognise gives the same result as 1.1.0; (d) Cancel leaves the project unchanged.
- REQ-095 (implemented): Matching the video's subtitles to the speech. Recognition runs on the range's audio at the subtitle quality level chosen in Settings (REQ-052); the recognised words are matched to the track's words ignoring case and punctuation, tolerating small spelling differences, missing words and extra words. Each line keeps the start and end of its cue; matched words get the recognised timing, kept inside the cue; unmatched words are spread evenly between their matched neighbours. The same rules as recognition apply: the speech model needs the consent dialog or a local file (REQ-014, REQ-052, REQ-053, REQ-058); the work runs in the background with the progress "Matching subtitles to the speech" in the Subtitles popup and the status bar; it follows "How hard videolore works" (REQ-050) and the window stays responsive (REQ-056). The track's text is never lost: in every fallback below the lines keep the track's text and cue timing, and the user is told.
  - A cue is not matched when less than half of its words are matched or there is no speech: it keeps its timing and its words are spread evenly; at the end a status message reads "N of M lines could not be matched to the speech; their words are spread evenly over the line".
  - On a processor that cannot run recognition (REQ-054), the dialog's button reads "Use the video's subtitles (this computer cannot match words to the speech; they will be spread evenly)"; every line keeps the track timing with words spread evenly; no download is offered.
  - If the user refuses the speech model download, the consent dialog offers an extra button "Use without matching words": every line keeps the track timing with words spread evenly; Cancel changes nothing.
  - If recognition fails, the whole range keeps the track timing with words spread evenly, and a message says so.
  - Cancelling during matching or during the download stops it and changes nothing: no lines, no undo step.
  - Acceptance (simulated recognition): (a) identical words get exactly the recognised timings; (b) with one misspelt, one missing and one extra word, the others are matched and the missing one is spread between its neighbours; (c) completely different text makes the cue fall back and the message counts it; (d) recognised times outside the cue are kept inside it; (e) on a simulated unsupported processor there is no download, every line falls back and nothing crashes; (f) Cancel during matching and during download leaves the project unchanged. A real-speech test with the Fast level on a reference clip with matching subtitles matches at least 90% of the words, each matched word starting within 0.3 s of the hand-checked reference. **Status in 1.2.0: this accuracy target is not met yet.** On the committed reference clip 23 of 28 words (82%) were matched, with word starts 0.48 s off on average and 1.10 s at worst. The strict test is therefore marked ignored; a weaker gated test checks that the text and cue times are kept and that every word is timed inside its line.

### Saving subtitles as a file
- REQ-088 (implemented): File > Export subtitles (.srt)… writes the lines as they are on the timeline: timeline times after cuts, trims and copies, numbered in time order, times as `HH:MM:SS,mmm --> HH:MM:SS,mmm`, UTF-8 without byte-order mark, one text line per cue. It is greyed out with "No subtitles on the timeline" when there are none. The save dialog proposes `<project name>.srt`. Positions and the video are not written; a failed or cancelled write leaves no partial file. Acceptance: (a) 3 lines, one in a copied clip, give 4 cues with timeline times; (b) a partly trimmed line has the cut times; (c) the file is read back correctly by a reference SRT reader; (d) the command is greyed out with 0 lines.

### Position on screen and selection
- REQ-089 (implemented): Each subtitle line has its own position on the picture; by default it is the style's usual place. Dragging the subtitle in the preview moves it; the centre of its text box stays on the picture, so the box can be dragged partly past an edge (REQ-117). The position is stored as a fraction of the picture, so it survives a change of output format (REQ-022), and is burned into the export at the same place: the centre is within 2% of the frame height of the preview position. Each finished drag is one undo step. The line's popup has "Reset position". The position belongs to the line, so copies of the same speech share it (REQ-018). Acceptance: (a) dragged to the top left, the exported text is at the top left; (b) dragging the centre past an edge stops at the edge, and a box dragged half out of the picture is cut in the preview and in the export (REQ-117); (c) undo restores the previous place; (d) Reset position restores the style's place; (e) other lines do not move.
- REQ-090 (implemented): Ctrl+click or Shift+click (Cmd+click on macOS for Ctrl) on a subtitle marker or a text item's bar on the Text lane, on its box in the preview, or on a line in the Subtitles popup list adds the object to the selection or removes it (a click without movement); a plain click selects only that object (REQ-077). Selected objects are highlighted on the timeline, in the list and in the preview. The selection stays until a plain click on an object not selected, a plain click on empty space (timeline or picture), or Esc when no popup is open (otherwise Esc first closes the topmost popup). Acceptance: Ctrl+click on 3 markers selects 3; Ctrl+click on one of them again leaves 2; Shift+click on a box in the preview adds it; a plain click leaves 1; a plain click on empty picture clears the selection.
- REQ-091 (implemented): Shift+drag on the timeline draws a time range and selects every subtitle line or text item whose timeline start is inside it (items that only end inside it are not selected). Shift+Ctrl/Cmd+drag adds to the selection. At the end of the drag the playhead moves to the start of the first selected item, and the selection is kept, so the selected items can be moved in the preview (REQ-092). Acceptance: with lines starting at 2, 5 and 9 s, Shift+drag from 4 to 10 s selects the 5 s and 9 s lines; a line from 3 to 6 s is not selected; afterwards the selection is still there.
- REQ-092 (implemented): With several objects selected, dragging any selected box in the preview moves the whole group: afterwards every selected line has exactly the dragged line's final position (their previous relative places are not kept). The selection stays after the drop. A group move is one undo step. Dragging a box that is not selected selects only it (REQ-090) and moves it. "Reset position" on a selection resets all of them. Acceptance: (a) 3 lines selected at different places (after a Shift+drag on the timeline), one dragged in the preview to 50% across and 20% down: all three are there and still selected; (b) one undo restores the 3 original places; (c) lines not selected do not move.

Subtitle quality levels, model downloads and storage, and older processors are covered by REQ-052, REQ-053 and REQ-054 in [Settings, performance and safety](business-performance.md).

### Placement and selection (2.0.3)
Applies to subtitle lines and to text items ([Text](business-text.md)). The accuracy of subtitle timing is in [Subtitle accuracy](business-subtitle-accuracy.md).
- REQ-168 (implemented): After a text edit that makes a box larger, a box that is now partly outside the picture is moved back inside by the least move that keeps it inside; a box wider or taller than the picture is centred on that axis. An edit that does not enlarge a box does not move it. The move is part of the edit: one undo step. Acceptance: a line whose right edge is 10% of its width outside gets a longer text: afterwards its right edge is on the picture edge; a shorter text leaves it where it is; one undo restores the old text and place.
- REQ-169 (implemented): Two subtitle lines shown at the same time whose boxes overlap in the preview are both marked with a warning in the Subtitles popup list and on the timeline; nothing moves by itself. The warning goes when the boxes or the times no longer overlap. The export is unchanged. Acceptance: two lines at one place at the same time are both listed with the warning; one dragged away clears it; the same place at different times gives no warning.
- REQ-170 (implemented): When several boxes overlap at the point clicked in the preview, the click selects the one of the lines shown at the playhead; each further click on the same point selects the next, in turn. A box is never unreachable because another covers it. Acceptance: two lines stacked at one place at the playhead: one click selects one, a second click on the same spot selects the other; a line shown at another time is not picked.
- REQ-171 (implemented): While a subtitle line or a text item is selected, the status bar shows a one-line hint of the selection gestures (Ctrl/Cmd+click adds or removes; Shift+drag on the timeline selects a time range), and the Subtitles popup lists them. The exact wording is an engineering choice. Acceptance: selecting one line shows the hint naming both gestures; with nothing selected no hint is shown.
- REQ-172 (implemented): With a selection of subtitle lines and text items together, dragging any selected box in the preview moves them all as one group, as REQ-092 does for lines: each selected item ends at the dragged item's final place. One undo step. Acceptance: a line and a text item selected, the text dragged to 50% across and 20% down: both end at that place; one undo restores both.
- REQ-173 (implemented): Align horizontally and Align vertically (REQ-098, REQ-115) work on a subtitle line still at its style's place: the line's own place on that axis becomes the centre of the picture, and the other coordinate stays. When every selected item is already centred on that axis, the command is greyed out with the reason "already centred" (REQ-099). Acceptance: a line at the style's place, Align horizontally: its centre is on the picture's vertical centre line; on a line already centred the command is greyed out with "already centred"; one undo restores the place.
- REQ-174 (implemented): When a subtitle line's text is enlarged (REQ-097) or wraps onto more lines, the centre of its box stays where it was; the box grows or shrinks around that centre, and REQ-168 applies. The preview and the export agree. Acceptance: a line with its centre at 50% across and 80% down gets a second line: its centre is still there within 0.5% of the picture height, in the preview and in the export.
- REQ-175 (implemented): A subtitle line or text item partly outside the picture shows a dashed outline in the preview, and the status bar reads "N items are partly outside the picture" (counted at the playhead). The Context Menu (REQ-106) and the Edit menu get **Move inside the picture** for the selection: each item moves by the least move that puts its whole box inside (centred on an axis if its box is larger than the picture). One undo step; greyed out when no selected item is partly outside. Acceptance: a line 30% outside shows the dashed outline and the status text; Move inside the picture puts its whole box inside; one undo restores its place; with every item inside the command is greyed out.
- REQ-176 (implemented): When Scale, Move or Change aspect (REQ-124 to REQ-131) leave a subtitle line or text item partly outside the picture, the item keeps its place in the frame (REQ-131) and REQ-175 applies; items do not follow the picture. Acceptance: a text at the right edge of a clip; Scale of 150% pushes part of it outside: the status text and the dashed outline appear; the item's place as a fraction of the frame is the same as before the Scale.

## Key decisions
- The speech model file must be exactly the expected one (checked before use), whether downloaded or provided by the user.
- The bundled subtitle font (Noto Sans Bold) covers Latin, Greek and Cyrillic scripts only.
- Changing the text of a line drops its per-word timing; the word-by-word highlight then spreads the words evenly over the line.
- If the same part of a source appears twice on the timeline (for example after copy and paste), editing the text of its subtitle changes both copies, because they are the same speech. The same holds for its position on screen.
- Any subtitle line in the project means the export is re-encoded.
- (1.2.0) When reusing a video's own subtitles, its text always wins and the start and end of each line are kept from the track; speech recognition is used only to time the individual words. If matching is impossible, the text is still used with words spread evenly, and the user is told.
- (1.2.0) A project still has one style; only the position can differ per line.
- (1.2.0) The `.srt` export is a separate command from the video export and uses the timeline times.
- (1.3.0) Selection is one thing across the timeline and the preview: a selection made with Shift+drag or Ctrl/Shift+click is kept while the selected boxes are dragged.
- (1.3.0) A subtitle line added at the playhead on a 360 clip is anchored to the point of the sphere the camera looks at, so it stays there when the camera turns (REQ-112 in [Boxes on the picture and 360 video](business-picture.md)).
- (2.0.3) Items are warned about (overlap, partly outside, REQ-169, REQ-175), not moved by themselves; the only automatic move is the one after a text edit that enlarges a box (REQ-168). Reason: a move the user did not ask for surprises them more than a warning. Items do not follow the picture when it is scaled or moved; they are warned (REQ-176).

## Known limitations
- Scripts other than Latin, Greek and Cyrillic (for example Chinese, Japanese, Arabic, Hindi) use whatever fonts the system has: in the exported video they may look different on each computer or appear as empty boxes, and they cannot be read in the application's subtitle editor.
- Recognition is done in pieces of about 2 minutes that overlap, so no word is lost or doubled at a boundary (REQ-178).
- x86-64 processors without AVX2 (from before about 2013) cannot generate subtitles automatically. Since 0.3.0 videolore checks this and shows a message instead of crashing (in 0.2.1 and 0.2.2 generation could crash on such processors). Since 1.2.0 a video's own subtitles can still be used there, with words spread evenly.
- (1.2.0) Matching a video's subtitles to the speech works only as well as the subtitle text follows what is actually said: translated or paraphrased tracks often fall back to evenly spread words. A message says so when at least half of the lines fall back (REQ-179).
- (1.2.0) Lines with their own position are not kept apart: two lines shown at the same time at the same place overlap. Both are marked with a warning (REQ-169); nothing moves by itself.
- (1.2.0) When a video's own subtitles are reused, their text and line times are kept exactly, but the word-by-word highlight timing is approximate: the accuracy target of REQ-095 (at least 90% of words matched, each within 0.3 s) is not met with the Fast level (23 of 28 words matched on the reference clip; the matched words start 0.48 s late on average). REQ-177 is still planned; see [Subtitle accuracy](business-subtitle-accuracy.md).
- (1.3.0) A subtitle line added on a 360 clip is drawn as plain white text in the preview and in the export: it has no preset box and no word-by-word highlight.

## Out of scope
- Positions in the `.srt` file (the `.ass` export has them).
- Planned for 2.0.4 (see [Subtitle files](business-subtitle-files.md)): WebVTT and ASS export, switchable subtitle tracks, importing `.srt` and `.vtt` files, fonts for more scripts.
- Picture-based subtitle tracks (DVD, Blu-ray, DVB): OCR not decided.
- Changing the start and end of a reused line from the speech (only words are matched).
- More than one language per project, translation.
- Custom style editor, a style per line or per clip.
- Actions on a selection of lines other than moving and resetting the position; snapping or safe-area guides.
- Speech models other than the three offered by the subtitle quality levels.
