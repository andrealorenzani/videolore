# Business — Export

Back to the [index](business.md).

## Features
- Export dialog (File > Export…, Ctrl/Cmd+E): mode (full video, only audio, only subtitles), file location and name, video format and audio format. Each format has "Same as source" as its reset.
- As fast as possible: no re-encoding when it is not needed; progress and cancel; a cancelled export leaves no partial file.
- Music, volumes and subtitles are included (subtitles and text items are burned into the picture).
- HEVC files play on Apple devices (tag hvc1).
- Subtitles can also be saved as a separate `.srt` file (REQ-088, see [Subtitles](business-subtitles.md)).
- A project whose clips are all 360 can export as 360 video or as a flat camera view (see [360 video](business-360-video.md)).
- **Faster export** with the computer's video hardware (NVIDIA, Intel, AMD, Apple), chosen in Settings; the normal method is the automatic fallback. Details: [Faster hardware export](business-hardware-export.md).

## Requirements
- REQ-021 (implemented): Export shows progress and can be cancelled; a cancelled export leaves no partial file.
- REQ-022 (implemented): The output format is chosen automatically unless the Export dialog chooses one (REQ-120, REQ-121): if all clips share the same format it is kept; otherwise the lowest by pixel count, then frame rate, then bitrate is used, with letterboxing and never stretching. With a Change aspect effect the frame size is the one that effect sets (REQ-125, see [Frame](business-frame.md)).
- REQ-023 (implemented): Export does as little processing as possible: stream copy (no re-encode) when all clips share an identical format, there are no effects (fades between clips or at the start or end of the video, subtitles, text items, Change aspect, Scale and Move (REQ-124 to REQ-131), music, video sound level other than 100% or muted, a clip volume other than 100% or a muted clip) and every cut starts on a keyframe, and the Export dialog keeps "Same as source" for both video and audio with no clip having Clean audio (REQ-114); otherwise a single re-encode pass, always frame-accurate.
- REQ-036 (implemented): Exported HEVC video in MP4 or MOV carries the Apple-compatible tag (hvc1), both with stream copy and with re-encode.
- REQ-037 (implemented): A timeline of 200 segments cut from one source exports successfully and frame-accurately.

### Export dialog (1.4.0)
- REQ-118 (implemented): **Export** (File > Export…, the Export toolbar button and Ctrl/Cmd+E) opens a dialog instead of exporting at once. Its **export file** field holds the location and the file name, with a "..." button for a folder chooser. The first time the name is the project's name in the project's folder; if the project has no folder yet, the folder of the last export of the session, else the user's documents folder. The extension follows the chosen format (.mp4, .mov, .m4a, .mp3 or .srt). The dialog has an **Export** button and Cancel. Export is disabled, with the reason shown, when the name is empty or invalid or the folder does not exist or cannot be written. If the file exists, a confirmation asks before it is replaced. The choices are kept for the session only; not in the project or in Settings. Acceptance: Ctrl/Cmd+E and File > Export… open the dialog; "..." changes the folder; a missing folder disables Export with its reason; an existing file asks first; Cancel writes nothing; a second opening in the same session shows the last choices.
- REQ-119 (implemented): The dialog's **mode** is **Full video** (REQ-021 to REQ-023 with the chosen formats), **Only audio** (the sound of the whole timeline as one audio file: video sound, music, volumes and Clean audio, in the audio format of REQ-121) or **Only subtitles (.srt)** (the file of REQ-088). Fields that do not apply are greyed out with a reason; Only subtitles is greyed out with "No subtitles on the timeline" when there are none. File > Export subtitles (.srt)… keeps working. Acceptance: Only audio of a 10 s timeline with music gives a 10 s audio file with the music at its volume; Only subtitles gives the same text as File > Export subtitles (.srt)….
- REQ-120 (implemented): **Video format** (Full video only) is a dropdown whose entries read like "1920x1080 25fps, libx264, .mp4" (plain codec names and the encoder tooltip per REQ-220). Its first entry, **Same as source**, is the automatic result of REQ-022, with stream copy when REQ-023 allows it. The other entries are a fixed list of 12 presets (resolution, frame rate, H.264 or HEVC, MP4 or MOV); a preset is offered only when the bundled ffmpeg has its encoder. A preset is always re-encoded and can use the hardware export (REQ-055). A 360 video (REQ-122) keeps the sphere's own size and ignores the preset. A small reset button sets "Same as source". Pictures keep letterboxing and are never stretched; HEVC in MP4 or MOV carries hvc1 (REQ-036). Acceptance: choosing 1280x720 25fps H.264 .mp4 gives a file of that size, frame rate and codec; reset returns "Same as source"; with "Same as source", identical clips and no effects, nothing is re-encoded.
- REQ-121 (implemented): **Audio format** (Full video and Only audio) is a dropdown with **Same as source** (the encoder of the source's audio codec, AAC by default; stream copy when REQ-023 allows; Only audio writes an .m4a file), **aac** (.m4a) and **mp3** (offered only when the bundled ffmpeg has its encoder). A small reset button sets "Same as source". A chosen audio format always re-encodes the audio. Acceptance: the aac file decodes with the timeline length; reset returns "Same as source"; mp3, when offered, gives an .mp3 file.
- REQ-122 (implemented): The **360 choice** (Full video only) appears only for a project whose video clips are all 360 clips: **360 video** or **Flat camera view** (REQ-103). A project without 360 clips and a mixed project show no 360 choice; a mixed project exports as a flat camera view, without a message. Acceptance: an all-360 project shows the choice; a mixed project and a project without 360 clips do not; the mixed project exports flat.

### Faster export (0.4.0)
- REQ-055 (implemented): Hardware export with silent fallback. When Export is "Faster" and the export needs re-encoding to H.264 or HEVC, videolore uses the working video hardware found by the background check (REQ-061): NVIDIA (Windows, Linux), Intel Quick Sync (Windows, Linux), AMD (Windows; on Linux AMD and Intel also through VA-API) and Apple VideoToolbox (macOS). If there is no working hardware, or the hardware run errors, crashes, makes no progress for 30 seconds, or fails the output check (REQ-063), the export is automatically redone with the normal method, with no error message. Acceptance (automated, simulated hardware, for each vendor): missing hardware, error exit, crash, stall and bad output each end with a finished file in the same format as Best quality with the correct number of frames; no error is shown; no temporary file remains (REQ-059). Cancelling during the hardware run, the check or the redo leaves no partial file (REQ-021) and never starts a redo. With Best quality the hardware is never used.
- REQ-060 (implemented): "Export" choice in Settings: **Export: Faster / Best quality**, with one-line explanations. Faster: "Uses your computer's video hardware when it can. Often quicker, but not on every computer; files may be a little larger." Best quality: "Uses the normal method. Slower, best picture for the file size." The recommendation is Best quality on every computer; Faster is opt-in and never greyed out; when the check found no usable hardware, its explanation adds "This computer has no video hardware videolore can use, so both choices give the same result." A change applies to the next export; a running export is not restarted. Acceptance: choosing Faster makes the status "Custom settings"; "Use recommended settings" (REQ-047) sets Best quality back; the choice is saved with the program settings, never in a project; the first start (REQ-045) saves Best quality.
- REQ-061 (implemented): Background hardware check. videolore checks in the background which video hardware works, separately for H.264 and HEVC, with a short test per candidate. Each candidate first gets 1 second; if that runs out of time (for example on a busy computer), the same candidate gets one more try of up to 4 seconds before the next candidate, so the preference order never changes. The whole check takes at most 10 seconds (waiting for a free process slot is not counted). If the answer is uncertain (no working hardware found for a format and a candidate ran out of time), the whole check is repeated once by itself; meanwhile the first answer is used. A test that runs out of time is never remembered as a failure (REQ-064). The check can be cancelled, never delays the window, start-up or preview, uses no network, and its processes count within the process limit (REQ-033). It runs only when needed: at start-up when the saved choice is Faster, when the user switches to Faster, on "Use recommended settings", on "Try video hardware again", and when the user-chosen ffmpeg changes while Faster is selected. With Best quality nothing runs. Preference order: Windows NVIDIA, then Intel, then AMD; Linux NVIDIA, then Intel Quick Sync, then VA-API; macOS VideoToolbox. Advanced shows which hardware works for each format and which method the last export used. Acceptance (simulated hardware): the time limits are respected (1 second first try, one 4-second retry per candidate, 10 seconds in total); hardware that answers only after the first second is still found and keeps its place; an uncertain answer triggers exactly one repeat; a timed-out test never turns the hardware off; the same set of working hardware always gives the same choice; a user-chosen ffmpeg (REQ-030) is checked the same way.
- REQ-062 (implemented): Same output as Best quality. A hardware export matches the Best quality result in container, video format, resolution, frame rate, picture format (8-bit 4:2:0), audio, number of frames and timing, letterboxing, fades, music and burned-in subtitles (REQ-022 and REQ-037 still hold). HEVC in MP4 or MOV carries hvc1 (REQ-036). The Export choice never changes whether stream copy is used (REQ-023): stream-copy exports are identical byte for byte with either choice. Formats other than H.264 and HEVC always use the normal method. Acceptance: stream-copy output identical under both choices; for each simulated vendor the format matches Best quality; real hardware is verified through QG-05.
- REQ-063 (implemented): Output check against silent hardware faults. After every hardware export, before the file appears at its destination, videolore checks that the whole file decodes without errors; that the number of frames, duration, resolution and frame rate are as expected; and that three sample frames (near the start, middle and end) are close to the same frames rendered with the normal method. If the check fails, the REQ-055 redo runs. Progress shows the check as part of the export. Acceptance: a simulated hardware run that writes an empty file, a truncated file, missing frames, the wrong picture size, or green or garbage frames is always detected and followed by a correct file made with the normal method; a correct hardware file passes.
- REQ-064 (implemented): Remember failing hardware. After a hardware failure for a format on this computer, later exports in that format go straight to the normal method, until the computer's characteristics change, videolore is updated, the check picks a different kind of hardware, the user presses "Use recommended settings" (which also runs the check again), or "Try video hardware again" in Advanced. Advanced shows "Video hardware turned off after a problem". A failure is remembered only when the redo with the normal method succeeded; cancelling never counts as a failure. Acceptance (simulated): after a failure the next export skips the hardware; each reset brings the hardware attempt back.
- REQ-065 (implemented): Upgrade from 0.3.x. A 0.3.x settings file without the Export choice loads with Export = Best quality, without any notice, and the "recommended / custom" status stays as it was. Upgrades from 0.2.x (REQ-049) and damaged settings (REQ-057) also get Best quality. Acceptance: a 0.3.0 settings file with recommended settings stays "Using recommended settings"; one with custom settings stays "Custom settings"; both load Export = Best quality.

The real-hardware check of this feature is quality goal QG-05, in [Settings, performance and safety](business-performance.md#quality-goals).

## Speed and format names (2.0.4)
- REQ-220 (implemented): The Export dialog's video entries use plain codec names ("H.264", "HEVC"); the encoder name (for example libx264) is in the entry's tooltip. Amends the entry names of REQ-120. Acceptance: no encoder names in the entries; the tooltip shows the encoder; the presets and their files are unchanged.
- REQ-221 (implemented): When the output frame's longer side is above 4096 px, the Export dialog shows a warning before Export: "This frame is larger than 4096 px: it is scaled down and the export takes longer." The export is still allowed and uses the cap of REQ-125. Acceptance: a 4K source in 9:16 shows the warning; 1920x1080 and 1600x2844 do not; the exported size is the capped one.
- REQ-222 (implemented): The blurred background of Fit empty with blurred video (REQ-126) is computed at one eighth of the frame size (divisor 8, chosen over the quarter first planned: at 4K 2.9 to 3.1 s against 3.3 to 3.7 s) and scaled up. The sharp picture is unchanged. Acceptance: the blurred background is computed at the reduced size (checked in a test); the sharp picture matches REQ-125 within 1 px.
- REQ-223 (implemented): In the preview, the blurred background is kept while its inputs (the frame, its size and the blur) do not change; a change of Scale, Move or keyframes redraws only the picture. Acceptance: ten Scale changes at one playhead draw the background once and the picture ten times (counted in a test); the preview equals the export within 2% of the frame height.
- REQ-224 (implemented): Import analysis, preview copy generation and subtitle preparation never delay the start of playback or of an export: they wait behind them in the queue of REQ-033. Acceptance: with ten imports queued, starting playback shows the first frame within 200 ms (test harness); an export started during imports begins before the imports finish.

## Key decisions
- Output format is automatic by default ("Same as source"). The Export dialog (REQ-118 to REQ-122) lets the user choose video and audio format, mode and location, replacing the earlier "no dialog" decision. The choices are fixed presets and "Same as source", not technical settings.
- Stream copy only when the result is guaranteed frame-accurate; correctness before speed. Changing the video audio volume or muting it also requires re-encoding, like music and subtitles.
- Best quality is the default everywhere; Faster is opt-in. Upgrades from 0.3.x load as Best quality.
- Every hardware result is checked (full decode, frame count and details, three sample frames), because some driver faults produce bad video without an error.
- Quality target for Faster: at most 3 VMAF points below Best quality, and files at most 1.5 times the Best quality size (checked by hand, QG-05).
- Hardware is used only for H.264 and HEVC (the broadest support). AV1 and VP9 and hardware decoding are in scope (Andrea, 2.0.2), built in 2.0.4 (REQ-212, REQ-213).
- Export keeps fixed presets and "Same as source"; hardware export always goes through ffmpeg (the bundled one or the one the user chose); no vendor pipeline (Andrea, 2.0.2).
- The hardware aims at the same average bitrate as the normal method. Exception, Linux VA-API: Ubuntu's free Intel driver refuses bitrate control, so VA-API uses constant quality with a size guard: a file over 1.5 times the expected Best quality size is redone with the normal method; this is not a hardware failure (REQ-064 does not remember it).
- The Faster explanation says "often quicker, but not on every computer", because on integrated graphics the hardware export plus its check was measured sometimes slower than Best quality (QG-05).
- The fallback is silent; the method used is shown in Settings, Advanced. The hardware check runs only when Faster is selected or asked for.
- The words "hardware" and "video hardware" are allowed in the main part of Settings; "GPU" and "encoder" are not.
- [MINOR] The REQ-221 warning is shown, not enforced; the 4096 px cap stays an assumption to be measured (QG-11).
- [MINOR] Codec names move to the tooltip (plain names for non-experts).

## Known limitations
- The format choices are a fixed list of presets; other resolutions, frame rates, bitrates or codecs cannot be chosen. The Export dialog remembers its choices for the session only.
- A 360 video ignores the video format presets; it keeps the sphere's own size.
- Frames larger than 4096 px export slower (warning only; not measured, see the [roadmap](roadmap.md)).
- Faster export limits (NVIDIA driver 610 or newer; Linux libva; RPM Fusion for Fedora; graphics cards; unverified systems; a 3-point output check; Faster not always quicker) are in [Faster hardware export](business-hardware-export.md).
- A driver fault that freezes the whole computer cannot be prevented by videolore.

## Out of scope
- Remembering export choices in the project or in Settings; custom resolutions, frame rates, bitrates, quality values or encoder options.
- Direct upload to social networks.
- Video hardware for effects or subtitles; preview copies stay on the normal method for encoding.
- Per-vendor quality sliders; AMD through its own interface on Linux; Intel Macs; ARM Windows and Linux; installing graphics drivers; a visible message when the fallback was used; making Faster the default; battery-aware behaviour.
